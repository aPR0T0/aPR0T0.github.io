LMC662 and ADC signal path reference

For LMC662AIMX/NOPB, SOIC-8: U1A pin 2 = −IN, pin 3 = +IN, pin 1 = OUT; supply pin 8 = +5F, pin 4 = −5F. U1B pin 5 receives the local 1.32 V reference; pin 6 sums through 40.2 kΩ from U1A output and 10 kΩ from pin 7. Pin 7 drives the protected/filtered ADC A0 input. ADC A1 receives local 1.65 V. All references are relative to FCOM.

ADS1115 differential A0−A1 at address 0x48, ±0.256 V and 128 SPS is the existing reference configuration. Differential measurement does not allow either analog pin outside the local 0–3.3 V supply range. Calibrate zero and gain in the actual assembly.

POWER & ENCLOSURE

Two shields, two different voltages.

The inner guard is at FCOM and inaccessible. The accessible outer case stays at PE. Choose a guarded feedthrough or appropriately rated triax arrangement with independent signal, guard and outer shield. Voltage withstand alone does not establish low leakage.

Specify insulation, spacings, discharge paths and transient protection for the complete assembly. Put supply bleeders on FCOM-to-PE as designed, never SUM-to-PE. Do not put an ordinary leaky clamp diode on SUM.

DATA & TIMING

A new acquisition handshake.

The grounded ESP32 sets X/Y and waits for settling. It sends a pixel-ID acquisition request over fibre. The floating MCU starts fresh ADC conversions and returns pixel ID, raw code and status. The ESP32 accepts the matching result before moving on.

Timeouts, stale IDs, clipping or loss of detector power must abort acquisition and request beam blanking. Existing direct-I²C firmware does not implement this link; optical isolation is not a beam safety interlock.

Calculation assumptions & limits

Positive net deposited-electron equivalent current gives VTIA−FCOM = InetRf. Conventional current has the opposite direction. The ADC differential change is negative because of the inverting conditioner. At ±0.256 V, its nominal 0.314 pA/code step is quantization, not sensitivity; the nominal uncalibrated zero is about −1.642 mV.

The leakage example uses residual SUM-to-chamber insulation, not guard-to-chamber insulation. Guard leakage is intended to bypass SUM. The ripple result is input displacement current before amplifier/ADC filtering; it is a sine-wave peak, not RMS broadband noise. The calculator does not combine DC offset with AC amplitude or model op-amp noise, drift, charging, field distortion, surface yields or image resolution. Baseline subtraction cannot remove random noise or changing leakage.

04 / VERIFY THE SIGNAL

Five checks before trusting a reading.

After a qualified assembly design, commission at zero bias with the beam blanked. Increase bias only after local power, protection and optical communication are established. Allow displacement currents to settle after every bias change.

  1. Calibrate within the floating section.

    Use an independently measured calibration resistor and quiet local voltage source referenced to FCOM: I = V / R. Check both polarities, gain and clipping at zero bias. For example, a +1 V local source through 100 MΩ injects +10 nA conventional current and drives the TIA negative. Remove the calibration source before electron collection.

  2. Repeat under intended bias.

    Keep the calibration entirely local; compare gain, offset, noise and settling at zero and elevated common potential. No grounded oscilloscope, USB cable, charger or programming lead may bridge the floating boundary.

  3. Measure the real collector background.

    Connect the feedthrough and collector, beam blanked. Log dark current and drift with pumps, gun heater, scan drivers and local digital/optical activity operating. Measure the full input capacitance and check for oscillation or slow recovery.

  4. Try to produce a false image.

    Run a raster with the beam blanked and then with electrons intercepted upstream. Scan-correlated structure identifies electrical pickup. Repeat with bias fixed; acquire after bias ramps have settled.

  5. Establish repeatable electron contrast.

    Start with a grounded conductive specimen. Compare repeated scans, measured beam currents and settled collector-bias settings. Record raw codes, dark zero, calibration, bias, timing and any overload or communication fault.

Example acceptance target

For an intended 10 pA contrast signal: aim for ≤1 pA RMS input-referred noise at the chosen pixel timing, with drift and systematic errors separately bounded below the useful contrast. These are proposed test targets. Nothing on this page establishes that the hardware meets them.

Acceleration improves attraction and changes landing energy; a plain metal collector has no intrinsic charge multiplication. Electrons that miss it or hit the guard are not counted. Electrons escaping the collector alter the net reading. Collector geometry, electric-field disturbance of the primary beam and an insulating specimen’s charging all still need investigation.
