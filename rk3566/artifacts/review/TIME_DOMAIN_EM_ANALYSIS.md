# Time-domain electromagnetic analysis: scope and native geometry evidence

This document reports the completed local RK3566 Rev 4 current and electromagnetic calculations, their independent geometry/method review, and the remaining work needed for an actual board field/current analysis. The whole PCB electromagnetic problem has not been solved. No schematic, PCB, BOM, or fabrication parameter was changed by this review.

The current extension combines a local U7 passive-network ngspice transient, an explicitly prescribed closed-current-path magnetic calculation, and a source-normalized two-dimensional grounded coplanar strip electric cross-section calculation. These are complementary conditional models. They do not form a coupled full-wave solution. The existing whole-board voltage envelope and its power-up timeline remain separate models.

## Completed numerical results

These results are from `simulation/data/time-domain.json` model version 1.1.0 and `simulation/data/electric-field.json` generated at `2026-09-14T11:56:32.554728+00:00`, both carrying the PCB hash above. The retained SPICE netlists and raw current/voltage CSV files are under `simulation/data/time-domain/`; full electric meshes are under `simulation/data/electric-field/`. Equal capacitor currents result from their identical lumped branches; the model does not establish real equal sharing through different package/PCB parasitics.

The **20.22 A cold result is not a predicted board startup current**. That deliberately unprotected ideal network exceeds U7's 6 A rated output and L219's 15.9 A saturation reference, specified for a 30% inductance reduction at 25 °C. Constant 2.2 µH behavior is consequently unsuitable for interpreting that extreme as a hardware prediction. Actual controller limiting, soft start and inductor nonlinearity were not modeled. Likewise, reducing the artifact with the programmed duty ramp does not prove actual startup success. The inductor rating is a specified test point, not an abrupt current threshold. [TI TPS566242 ratings](https://www.ti.com/product/TPS566242), [Coilcraft XAL60xx data, XAL6030-222](https://www.coilcraft.com/getmedia/ea51f14b-7f32-4dc6-8dfe-d4b70549040f/xal60xx.pdf)

For the last-20-cycle settled measurement window, calculated L219 DCR loss is 56.715 mW and each output capacitor's assumed ESR loss is 44.349 µW. These exclude core, switching, dielectric and thermal effects. Across all four full runs, maximum KCL residual is 0.674 µA, maximum capacitor charge-balance error is 2.482 pC, and maximum absolute energy-balance residual is 45.62 nJ. Halving the maximum SPICE step from 20 to 10 ns changes reported output-voltage peaks by at most 0.000323% and inductor-current peaks by at most 0.001019%; adaptive source breakpoints resolve the prescribed edges. These are numerical consistency checks, not validation of the unavailable controller. The later video thermal illustration uses a separate last-five-cycle mean of 56.705 mW; see `simulation/VIDEO_THERMAL_METHOD.md` for its assumptions.

Halving mesh spacing changes the largest fixed-probe field by 1.364% and capacitance by 0.308%. Expanding the grounded remote boundaries to ±3 mm and 1.61 mm height on the coarse mesh changes the largest probe by 1.985% and capacitance by 0.0938%. Those meet the declared 5% mesh/2% boundary screens; they are not an absolute field-error bound. The discrete normalized potential residual is `1.56×10⁻¹² V`. Independent integration on the coarse retained mesh gives 208.105515525 pF/m from trace charge and 208.105515260 pF/m from field energy, relative disagreement `1.28×10⁻⁹`. Analytical parallel-plate, layered-dielectric flux, and symmetric coplanar-boundary tests also pass. Central-difference E exactly at a dielectric interface is an average across the jump; the fixed probes above avoid that ambiguity.

**Local current-density and capacitive-current scales.** The representative native trace's proposed `0.300 mm × 0.035 mm` copper cross-section is 0.0105 mm². Assigning the modeled L219 current through that segment gives the cross-section average `Javg(t)=IL(t)/0.0105 mm²`: 150.33–230.59 A/mm² during the settled window, with an RMS value of 191.89 A/mm². This is a signed-current/area diagnostic; actual current crowds nonuniformly with position and frequency. With assumed bulk resistivity `ρ=1.724×10⁻⁸ Ω·m` at 20 °C, its 1.1 mm length has a geometric DC resistance estimate of 1.8061 mΩ. The corresponding `Irms²Rdc` scale is 7.332 mW and the peak `IRdc` scale is 4.373 mV. Those quantities do not include spreading, roughness, temperature rise, skin/proximity effects or the rest of the route, and were not fed back into the current-producing SPICE model.

Multiplying the solved cross-section capacitance by the 1.1 mm straight length gives an **extruded-section estimate** of 0.228214 pF. For the prescribed 5 V change in 2 ns, `i=C′ℓ·dVsw/dt` gives a 0.5705 mA edge-current scale. It omits finite-length/package capacitance and does not divide charge among the coplanar and buried grounds. This diagnostic is not an additional solved branch in the SPICE KCL results. A coupled distributed model must provide those return currents, parasitic inductance and frequency-dependent current density.

These values are fields of the **declared route hypotheses**, not measured fields or bounds on the board's emissions. Moving the assumed return to a remote depth changes individual unit-current probe kernels in different directions: for example, the C382 coefficient changes from 9.207 to 30.600 µT/A, while C384 changes from 148.830 to 130.126 µT/A. This demonstrates model sensitivity; the larger value is not a guaranteed worst case. That norm is a plane integral of squared unit-current kernels, not magnetic energy. No magnetic value from the out-of-range cold experiment qualifies as a physical prediction.

The practical findings are that the declared passive U7 network has small settled ripple, an ideal abrupt load change produces a materially larger voltage excursion, and startup behavior depends strongly on omitted controller/nonlinear behavior. Local conductor geometry supports a grounded coplanar cross-section, but return-path choices materially affect calculated magnetic fields. None of these results qualifies RK3566 boot, EMI/EMC compliance, actual current density, or the full-board startup sequence.

## U7 electric cross-section: actual coverage and a material caveat

The longest straight `SBC_SW_3V3` segment is on B.Cu, UUID `28861715-7d86-401a-9f54-768491228f45`. It runs from `(126.15, 107.90)` to `(126.15, 109.00)` mm, length 1.100 mm, width 0.300 mm. Its center is `(126.15, 108.45)` mm, or board-local `(5.15, 30.95)` mm. A transverse cross-section runs along global x at `y=108.450` mm.

Boolean intersection of the saved In6 GND fill with the straight segment's rectangular footprint, `x=126.000…126.300`, `y=107.900…109.000` mm, gives **0.330000 mm² of 0.330000 mm² coverage**. This excludes round end caps. At the center cross-section, the continuous In6 GND interval is `x=121.500000…131.095141444` mm, extending 4.650 mm left and 4.945 mm right of the trace center. This supports a local ground-plane approximation under this segment. It does not establish the complete current return distribution.

The same cross-section also intersects **B.Cu ground on both sides of the trace**:

| Conductor at `y=108.450 mm` | Global x interval, mm | Gap from the switch trace |
|---|---:|---:|
| Left B.Cu GND fill | 122.050500…125.890681628 | 109.318 µm |
| Switch trace | 126.000000…126.300000 | — |
| Right B.Cu GND fill | 126.410419192…128.913601375 | 110.419 µm |
| In6 GND fill | 121.500000…131.095141444 | 75 µm dielectric below B.Cu |

Consequently, the selected electric case is a **grounded coplanar strip** and includes these coplanar conductors at their extracted offsets. An isolated strip over a ground plane would be a simplified comparison only, since omitting the side grounds changes local fringing fields and capacitance. Even including them still assumes longitudinal invariance and a grounded plane without finite conductivity or return voltage. Trace ends, the diagonal approach, package pads, bends, solder mask, and dielectric construction are absent from that two-dimensional model.

The complete switch net contains five B.Cu tracks, total centerline length 4.378161172 mm, and no vias. The five lengths are 1.100000, 1.060660172, 1.050001, 0.830000, and 0.337500 mm. Two are 1.000 mm wide; the other three are 0.300 mm wide. The selected 0.300 mm cross-section therefore cannot be applied to every point on the switch net.

These facts identify regions to analyze; they are not measured emission failures. A solved sheet-current distribution requires finite conducting copper, ports and source currents, connected vias, and appropriate boundaries. An arbitrary shortest path or a single image wire is a declared return approximation.

## What the time-domain extension may report

| Quantity | Defensible current scope | Must remain explicitly unknown |
|---|---|---|
| Branch voltage/current | U7 lumped source, L219, individual C382–C385 branches and declared load, solved by ngspice | Actual IC switching waveforms, all other IC pin currents, firmware-dependent activity |
| Power and energy | Signed terminal power, ESR/DCR dissipation, stored L/C energy and time integrals for modeled branches | IC switching/core/thermal loss without applicable device models |
| Magnetic B and H | Biot–Savart finite-wire kernel for a fully specified closed route, scaled by its prescribed branch current; `H=B/µ0` in air | Solved PCB surface-current density, package/core fields, all return-path sharing, radiated emissions |
| Electric E | `E=-∇φ` from a defined 2D dielectric/conductor boundary problem, normalized at 1 V and scaled by `Vsw(t)` | Inductive E, end fringing, propagation, whole-board coupled E/B solution, measured dielectric stress |
| Whole-board startup | Existing averaged rail envelope and explicit assumed timing | RK3566 firmware execution, DDR training, real boot success |

For a charge-free, linear dielectric cross-section, solve `∇·(ε∇φ)=0` with the switch conductor at 1 V and declared grounded conductors at 0 V. With fixed geometry and material, the scalar electric contribution obeys `E(r,t)=Vsw(t) E1(r)`. Permittivity and all outer boundary conditions must be recorded. Two-dimensional electrostatics assumes invariance along the omitted direction; a moving time cursor does not remove that assumption. [COMSOL electrostatics equations](https://doc.comsol.com/6.4/doc/com.comsol.help.edis/edis_ug_acdc.7.71.html)

The magnetic calculation uses the specified current route as an input; it does not discover the route. Include each component/package bridge and return segment in the exported geometry, including layer elevation. A finite-wire singularity requires a stated probe exclusion distance or a validated finite-cross-section formulation. B is in tesla; H is in A/m. Air permeability cannot represent L219's shield/core. Biot–Savart current sources and conducting boundaries are separate parts of a magnetic model. [MIT, Electromagnetic Fields and Energy, Chapter 8, particularly §8.2, §8.4 and §8.6](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/res-6-001-electromagnetic-fields-and-energy-spring-2008/pages/chapter-8/)

The separate E and B calculations cannot be combined into a radiated-power result or a plane-wave `E/H=377 Ω` conversion. The total electric field also contains the inductive contribution `-∂A/∂t`, omitted by the scalar cross-section solve. A complete transient magnetic formulation retains Faraday coupling. [COMSOL magnetic/electric potentials and quasistatic theory](https://doc.comsol.com/6.1/doc/com.comsol.help.comsol/COMSOL_ReferenceManual.pdf)

## Time scales and quantitative limits

The existing 5 µs rail-envelope timestep cannot resolve individual 600 kHz, 2 MHz or 3 MHz switching cycles, much less nanosecond edges. Detailed local transients must use a separately declared short time window, maximum timestep, source rise/fall times, and initial conditions. A fixed PWM source and initialized output network are not a TPS566242 startup or feedback-control model.

As an engineering **screening choice**, use `kL=2πfL/v ≤ 0.1` to flag regions where retardation is small. This is deliberately conservative and is not a universal error bound or certification rule. With assumed isotropic `εr=4.2`, `µr=1`, `v=c/√εr=146.284×10⁶ m/s`:

| Characteristic size L | Frequency at `kL=0.1` | `kL` at 175 MHz |
|---|---:|---:|
| 77.782 mm board diagonal | 29.93 MHz | 0.585 |
| 10.000 mm local region | 232.82 MHz | 0.0752 |
| 4.378 mm switch-track length | 531.77 MHz | 0.0329 |

The commonly used edge bandwidth estimate `0.35/tr` is 175 MHz for an assumed 2 ns edge; it is a heuristic, not a spectral cutoff. The entire closed route and probe region, not just the SW metal length, determine the relevant L. Small electrical size alone does not establish the suitability of neglecting induction or displacement currents. Quasistatic methods suppress propagation delay under their respective assumptions. [COMSOL electrical-size and formulation guidance](https://doc.comsol.com/6.3/doc/com.comsol.help.acdc/acdc_ug_modeling.06.03.html)

Using the ideal good-conductor relation `δ=√(2/(ωµσ))`, with assumed copper conductivity `σ=5.8×10⁷ S/m` and `µ=µ0`, skin depths are 85.32 µm at 600 kHz, 46.73 µm at 2 MHz, 38.15 µm at 3 MHz, and 5.00 µm at 175 MHz. These are screening calculations, not extraction of actual current density: geometry, proximity, plating, surface roughness and temperature also matter. The high-frequency result is smaller than both the 18 and 35 µm foils. A uniform-current centerline approximation cannot establish actual local copper current density at those frequencies.

Ideal conductor corners produce mesh-dependent field peaks. Report fixed-distance probes, a declared averaging region, and mesh convergence; mask conductor interiors and singular neighborhoods. A raw maximum at a sharp ideal corner is not a physical dielectric-breakdown or EMC margin. Field dump/probe coordinates and interpolation must be explicit. [openEMS mesh and field sampling guidance](https://docs.openems.de/en/latest/concepts/mesh.html)

Brute-force full-board FDTD over the 160 ms startup envelope is inappropriate for this geometry. As an illustrative uniform three-dimensional air-grid calculation, the standard CFL expression gives a maximum step about 0.144 ps for 75 µm cells, requiring about `1.11×10¹²` steps for 160 ms; 18 µm cells are more restrictive. This is a computational scale illustration, not a proposed mesh. A practical fuller analysis should extract passive port models or solve short switching windows with qualified excitations. [openEMS CFL discussion](https://docs.openems.de/en/latest/concepts/mesh.html)

## Numerical acceptance checks and web presentation

The completed EM-01 datasets contain the local checks and convergence evidence summarized above. Preserve these acceptance checks when changing the models; they do not replace the missing physical evidence listed in EM-02 onward.

1. **Circuit:** retain individual capacitor currents; check per-node KCL, sum of capacitor currents, signed source/load power and storage/loss energy balance. Compare smaller maximum timestep and tighter tolerances, including switching edges. RMS and integrals must use actual timestep intervals. Mark assumed ESR, DCR, capacitance bias, edge times and initial conditions.
2. **Magnetic kernel:** compare analytical finite-wire/closed-loop benchmarks; check coordinate units, reversal, superposition and route closure. Compare return-path variants without describing the envelope as a guaranteed physical bound. Export exact route points and distinguish native metal from invented bridges/returns. Check probes outside the conductor exclusion radius.
3. **Electric solver:** check analytic parallel-plate and layered-dielectric cases, potential bounds, boundary enforcement, equation residual, dielectric flux continuity and capacitance from energy/charge. Compare smaller mesh and expanded outer boundaries at fixed physical probes. Record whether coplanar ground is included. A mesh-converged incorrect boundary model still represents that incorrect boundary assumption.
4. **Displayed result:** show source hash, case, time and units, geometry/return assumptions, solver versus prescribed-source status, probe height/distance and convergence evidence. Label omitted regions as unavailable, not zero. Keep rail-envelope time separate from the local microsecond/nanosecond window. If a component lacks a current model, keep its value unknown rather than interpolating a board heatmap.

The current exercise has no measured field evidence, no full-wave board solution, no RK3566 firmware emulation, and no qualified models for the 16 ICs previously audited. The historical nominal behavior score of 9/10 and broader evidence score of 2.85/10 refer to the existing declared rubric. Its broader evidence score is structurally capped below 8 until missing evidence is supplied. New local plots must not silently increase that score or change the release criterion. A future evidence-rich rubric must be versioned and agreed before rescoring, preserving historical results. The reproducible handoff for that work is `simulation/NEXT_AGENT_HANDOFF.md`.

## Solver reproduction and raw-data provenance

Run the following commands **from `rk3566-sbc-rev4/simulation/`** using the installed Python with `pcbnew` and `libngspice.so.0` available, plus `g++` for the electric solver. Keep `data/board.json` and `data/board-layout.json` matched to the native PCB; follow the main simulation README's extraction steps first if the native revision changes. These commands regenerate simulation artifacts only; they do not modify or save the hardware design.

```sh
python3 em_geometry_audit.py
python3 electric_field.py
python3 time_domain.py
python3 -m unittest -v test_electric_field test_time_domain
```

The electric command compiles the C++ source into a temporary, source-hashed executable and runs the coarse, fine and expanded-domain cases. The time-domain command runs four base experiments and four comparisons with half the maximum timestep. The unit tests independently check the analytical electrical/magnetic cases and model bookkeeping. Re-running a solver creates a new derived result; preserve the earlier datasets if comparing model revisions.

## Geometry reproduction

Run from the `rk3566-sbc-rev4` directory using the installed Python that provides `pcbnew`. This reads saved copper only and does not refill or save the board. It verifies the representative segment and exact rectangular In6 ground coverage; the SHA assertion prevents silent reuse on a changed revision.

Regenerate the full machine-readable evidence with `python3 simulation/em_geometry_audit.py`. The output contains all native ground intervals, the two neighboring coplanar conductors in the field solver's local coordinates, native source and extractor hashes, zone UUIDs, exact reference coverage, and proposed stack dimensions. The following independent short check verifies the selected native segment and its reference coverage.

```sh
python3 - <<'PY'
from pathlib import Path
import hashlib
import pcbnew

path = Path('hardware/rk3566-sbc.kicad_pcb')
assert hashlib.sha256(path.read_bytes()).hexdigest() == (
    '3ba4da7036173e31941ea22b041d1a2a3433c2bcdedccf1a75b1f52b31ce2807')
board = pcbnew.LoadBoard(str(path))
uid = '28861715-7d86-401a-9f54-768491228f45'
track = next(t for t in board.GetTracks() if t.m_Uuid.AsString() == uid)
print(track.GetNetname(), board.GetLayerName(track.GetLayer()),
      pcbnew.ToMM(track.GetWidth()), pcbnew.ToMM(track.GetLength()))
rectangle = pcbnew.SHAPE_POLY_SET()
rectangle.NewOutline()
for x, y in [(126.0,107.9), (126.3,107.9),
             (126.3,109.0), (126.0,109.0)]:
    rectangle.Append(pcbnew.FromMM(x), pcbnew.FromMM(y))
area = 0.0
for zone in board.Zones():
    if zone.GetNetname() != 'GND':
        continue
    if not zone.GetLayerSet().Contains(pcbnew.In6_Cu):
        continue
    intersection = pcbnew.SHAPE_POLY_SET(
        zone.GetFilledPolysList(pcbnew.In6_Cu))
    intersection.BooleanIntersection(rectangle)
    area += intersection.Area()/1e12
print('In6 GND intersection area, mm^2:', area)
assert abs(area-0.330000) < 1e-8
PY
```

Cross-section ground intervals above were obtained by intersecting every saved polygon-outline edge with `y=108.450 mm`, testing each adjacent x interval with native `SHAPE_POLY_SET.Contains`, and merging touching intervals. That process retains voids represented by fractured outlines. When rerunning on another PCB revision, recompute both the In6 and B.Cu intervals before reusing the field model.
